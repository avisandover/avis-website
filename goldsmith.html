Handicapped use:

AVIS completed an All Persons Trail from the parking area out to Bessie’s Point, about 7/10 of a mile, in 2026. The trail meets current Forest Service Trail Accessibility Guidelines (FSTAG). The surface material is crushed stone.

Natural features

The property is a glacial outwash plain ribbed with prominent eskers that direct rainfall into two separate river basins: the Ipswich River and the Shawsheen River.

After watching the Civilian Conservation Corps plant thousands of red pine in Harold Parker Forest, Bessie and her brother planted a large stand of red pine at Goldsmith. White pine is gradually becoming dominant, with hemlock appearing in the understory. Oak and red maple are also plentiful.

The marshes adjacent to the pond are home to cattails, pitcher plants, sundew, snapping turtles, and great blue herons. In late March, the woods frogs sing in the vernal pools. In May, you will see plentiful lady slippers along the trails. The uplands provide habitat for resident deer, ﬁsher, and wild turkey, while owls patrol the margins.

History

Goldsmith Woodlands is rich in history. Relics of an Indian encampment have been found near Foster’s Pond, and a large number of projectile points (arrowheads) and flakes have been discovered in several locations around Foster’s Pond.

Foster’s Pond was probably named for Andrew Foster, the 22nd man to settle in Andover. He died in 1685 at the age of 106. His wife, Ann Foster, was one of 41 people accused of witchcraft during 1692 and 1693.

An 1830 map shows the Goldsmith family as owners of the woodlands. During the early 20th century, there were 9 cabins on the property. Bessie’s cabin was located on Bessie’s Point, with the approach road lined with rhododendrons and mountain laurel that she planted.

Another cabin belonged to Zack, a caretaker and handyman on the property. His name lives on in the designation of Zack’s way, Zack’s house, and Zack’s hop pit, where a visitor can still see the old cellar holes.

In 1974, Bessie Goldsmith, a dedicated conservationist, left her woodlands and land inherited from her father and her brother Clarence, in trust to the Fund for Preservation of Wildlife and Natural Areas, now part of the Boston Foundation. AVIS became the caretaker in 1977, with funds for maintenance provided by Bessie’s trust. In 2000, The Boston Foundation provided funding to help AVIS acquire the house and land at 491 South Main Street and create some off-street parking. In 2025, the Boston Foundation transferred the 140 acres left in trust by Bessie Goldsmith to AVIS.

Suggested walks

Goldsmith Extended Loop Trail: Follow the High Trail to each of three lookouts over Foster’s Pond, then return via Clarence’s Trail and Zack’s Way: 3.7 miles, 167 feet of total elevation, steep in spots.

All Persons’ Trail to Bessie’s Point: (1.5 miles round-trip). Follow the main trail to the beautiful overlook at Bessie’s Point. The 6-foot-wide crushed stone trail has a maximum slope of 7%, with most of it under 5%.
