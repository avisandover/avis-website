Amy Gordon Taft Reservation

History

During the 17th century, the land now comprising the Taft Reservation belonged to the Bradstreet family. Anne Dudley Bradstreet was the first published English-language poet in the New World. On a darker note, public executions were held nearby during the colonial era. In 1689, Hugh Stone was hanged for murdering his wife, the mother of his six children.

In 1748, Henry Gray II built the first mill on the site, using a branch of the Skug River to grind scythes. His descendant Henry Jenkins Gray added a sawmill in 1853, which remained until its demolition in the 1930s for a Civilian Conservation Corps camp. Broken millstones and rusted hoops still lie along the stream.

The Taft name came later, when the Rev. Arthur Taft, a retired Episcopal priest from Colorado, settled in Andover. His family bought the Gray property in 1932; his three children inherited it in 1967. Rebekah Longwood Taft, a conservationist and AVIS trustee, donated 30 acres in memory of her mother, Amy Gordon Taft, to be preserved forever in its natural 14 state. AVIS’s 1997 purchase of 19 adjoining acres and the Town’s later transfer of a Coventry Lane parcel expanded the reservation to 62 acres.

Traces of the land’s farming past remain—stone walls, ox-cart ruts, a sunken aqueduct once channeling water to a cow pond, an old apple orchard, and rusting buckets from its days as a working dairy farm.

Natural features

Along the trails grow oak, maple, aspen, hornbeam, birch, tall cedar, and jack pine, some marked with metal labels. The reservation is noted for its wildflowers—lady’s slippers, jack-in-the-pulpit, Indian pipes, cardinal flowers, starflowers, Solomon’s seal, and many others cataloged by Andover wildflower expert John Gould. Escaped domestic vinca carpets the northern area, competing with native poison ivy, while partridgeberries and club mosses cover the ground elsewhere. Ferns and skunk cabbage thrive in wet areas, and the meadow near Coventry Lane, often flooded, supports a rich variety of shrubs and plants.
